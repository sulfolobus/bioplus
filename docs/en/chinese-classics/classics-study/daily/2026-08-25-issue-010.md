## Reading guide (English)

### Triple Burner Meridian (Hand-Shaoyang)

Focus: the classical "route" description (from ring finger → wrist → outer arm → shoulder → supraclavicular fossa → chest/diaphragm → triple burner, with branches to ear and outer eye corner), and its conceptual role as the system that "connects" the whole body's qi and fluid circulation.

### Dao De Jing — Chapter 10

Focus: the unity of body and spirit, and the deepest virtue of "creating without possessing." Key themes:

- Holding body and soul as one (抱一) — can you keep them from drifting apart?
- Gathering qi to utmost softness — can you be like a baby?
- Cleaning the deep mirror of the mind — can you be without flaw?
- Governing with no cleverness (无知).
- "Giving life and nurturing, yet not possessing, not claiming, not dominating" — this is Called Profound Virtue (玄德).

The practice implication is a creator's mindset: produce and nourish, but do not cling to ownership or credit.

### Kṣitigarbha Sutra — Section 10 (Weighing the Merit of Giving)

Focus: this section explains why the same act of giving yields vastly different merits. The decisive factors are (a) the attitude — humility, a sincere smile, giving with one's own hands to the lowest and most afflicted; and (b) the scope of dedication — dedicating merit to all beings (法界) vs. only to one's own family. The wider the heart, the larger the return.

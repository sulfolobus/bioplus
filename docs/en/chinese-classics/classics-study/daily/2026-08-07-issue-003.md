## Reading guide (English)

### Stomach Meridian (Foot-Yangming)

Focus: the longest of the twelve regular meridians. Its classical "route" starts at the face (beside the nose → upper teeth → lips → forehead), passes the chest and abdomen (connecting the stomach and networking the spleen), then runs down the front-lateral thigh, knee, and shin to the dorsum of the foot and the second toe, with a branch reaching the big toe to hand off to the next meridian.

Conceptual hint: digestion is not "only a stomach matter" — it is strung along an entire line from face to foot, and the stomach–spleen pair work as one team.

### Dao De Jing — Chapter 3

Focus: reducing externally-driven comparison and desire.

- "Do not exalt the worthy" / "do not prize rare goods" / "do not display temptations" — the point is the act of放大 (elevating, pricing, showing), which stirs competition and restlessness.
- The ideal governance/person: "empty the heart, fill the belly, soften the will, strengthen the bones" — a settled, sufficient life rather than one driven by status.
- "Govern by non-action (为无为), and nothing is left ungoverned" — enable the right things to happen instead of over-managing.

### Kṣitigarbha Sutra — Section 3 (karma and the Avīci)

Focus: Queen Māyā asks on behalf of ordinary beings how different actions meet different results. Kṣitigarbha answers with a blunt account of heavy karmas (e.g., unfiliality, slandering the Triple Gem) and then defines the five meanings of "Avīci / uninterrupted" (无间): uninterrupted in time, in space, in instruments of suffering, in recipients (no exception by status), and in life-span (countless deaths and rebirths until the karma is exhausted).

Conceptual hint: read "Avīci" as the *irreversibility and continuity of karma* — small neglects accumulate, and so do small consistent goods.

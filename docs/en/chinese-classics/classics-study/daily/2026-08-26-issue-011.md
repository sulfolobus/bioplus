## Reading guide (English)

### Gallbladder Meridian (Foot-Shaoyang)

Focus: the "side-body" route. Starting from the outer canthus of the eye, the meridian sweeps the temple, runs behind the ear, down the side of the neck and shoulder, then — via a branch that crosses the diaphragm — networks the liver and belongs to the gallbladder (the liver–gallbladder pairing), continues along the lateral chest and ribs, the outer hip, outer knee, front of the outer ankle, across the dorsum of the foot, and ends at the fourth toe. Signature signs in the classical text: bitter taste in the mouth (口苦) and frequent sighing (善太息) — markers of constrained qi. Its terminal branch reaches the big toe, where the Liver Meridian begins — the direct setup for the next issue.

### Dao De Jing — Chapter 11

Focus: the usefulness of emptiness. Three concrete images carry the argument:

- thirty spokes converging on one hub — it is the hub's **empty center** that lets the cart work;
- a clay vessel — its value lies in the **hollow** that can hold things;
- a room with doors and windows — the **openings and inner space** make it habitable.

The chapter closes with the formula: "what is there provides the advantage; what is not there provides the use" (有之以为利，无之以为用). The practice implication: protect the empty center that lets a system actually run — a full schedule with no slack, or a team with no margin for error, loses the very space where thinking and correcting happen.

### Kṣitigarbha Sutra — Section 11 (The Earth Spirit Protects the Dharma)

Focus: the Earth Goddess (坚牢地神) opens by praising Kṣitigarbha's vows as countless as the sands of a thousand million Ganges rivers — deeper even than those of Mañjuśrī, Samantabhadra, Avalokiteśvara and Maitreya, whose vows "still have an end." She then offers a strikingly grounded practice: enshrine the bodhisattva's image in a clean spot at one's own dwelling (traditionally the south side), and gain ten benefits centered on security — fertile land, lasting peace of the home, ancestors reborn in heaven, longevity for the living, wishes fulfilled, freedom from flood and fire, depletion averted, nightmares stopped, protection in coming and going, and frequent encounters with noble causes. The Buddha confirms her role: everything that grows from the earth depends on her power, and whoever practices even one thing from this sutra is guarded so that "calamity does not even reach their ears."

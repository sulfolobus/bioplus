## Reading guide (English)

### Pericardium Meridian (Hand-Jueyin)

Focus: the classical "route" description (from chest → pericardium → diaphragm → triple burner → armpit → inner arm between two tendons → middle finger), and its conceptual role as the "outer guard" of the heart — protecting the core while maintaining necessary connections.

### Dao De Jing — Chapter 9

Focus: the wisdom of stopping and retreating at the right time. Key themes:

- Holding something until it is full is less wise than stopping in time.
- Sharpening to extreme sharpness cannot be preserved long.
- Gold and jade filling the hall cannot be guarded forever.
- Wealth and honor with arrogance bring self-inflicted calamity.
- Withdrawing after accomplishment is the way of Heaven.

The practice implication is recognizing the natural limits of any cycle and knowing when to step back rather than pushing forward indefinitely.

### Kṣitigarbha Sutra — Section 9 (Chanting the Buddha's Names)

Focus: this section marks a shift from narrative to practical method — listing the names of past Buddhas and the merit of chanting them. The core message is that a single moment of reverent attention can begin a profound transformation, and that chanting a Buddha's name at the time of death can help dissolve accumulated karma.

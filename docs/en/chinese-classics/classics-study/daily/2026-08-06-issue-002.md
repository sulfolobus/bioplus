## Reading guide (English)

### Large Intestine Meridian (Hand-Yangming)

Focus: the meridian's route begins at the index finger tip (connecting with the Lung Meridian which ends at the thumb), passes through Hegu (合谷, the famous acupoint between thumb and index finger), travels up the outer arm to the shoulder, enters the supraclavicular fossa, connects with the lung, and belongs to the large intestine. A branch ascends through the neck and face, crossing at the philtrum (人中) left-to-right and right-to-left, reaching the opposite nostril.

Key concept: the "exterior-interior pairing" (表里) of lung and large intestine — they share one meridian line, which is why TCM theory treats respiratory and digestive rhythms as interconnected.

### Dao De Jing — Chapter 2

Focus: the relativity of all value judgments. When the world defines "beauty," "ugliness" arises simultaneously; when "good" is defined, "not-good" follows. Six pairs (being/non-being, difficult/easy, long/short, high/low, sound/voice, front/behind) are mutually arising.

The sage's practice: act without forcing ("wu-wei"), teach without words, create without possessing, accomplish without claiming credit. "Because he does not dwell on his success, it does not depart."

### Kṣitigarbha Sutra — Section 2 (Assembly of Emanations)

Focus: Kṣitigarbha's countless emanation-bodies (分身) from innumerable worlds gather at Trāyastriṃśa Heaven. The Buddha formally entrusts the beings of the Sahā world to Kṣitigarbha until Maitreya's appearance. Key themes:

- The Buddha describes his own painstaking efforts, using countless forms to reach different types of beings.
- Kṣitigarbha vows: "Even a hair, a drop, a grain of sand, a speck of dust — if it is a good deed done within the Buddha-Dharma, I will gradually guide and deliver."
- He repeats three times: "May the World-Honored One not worry about the beings of future ages who commit evil."
- The Buddha praises him: "Excellent! Excellent! I rejoice with you."

## Reading guide (English)

### Liver Meridian (Foot-Jueyin)

Focus: the longest-reaching route in the twelve-meridian cycle. Starting from the big toe — exactly where the Gallbladder Meridian's terminal branch ended (the "三毛" region) — the Liver Meridian runs along the medial dorsum of the foot, passes behind the Spleen Meridian on the inner leg, ascends the medial thigh, enters the pubic region, encircles the genitals, reaches the lower abdomen, traverses the stomach, belongs to the liver and networks the gallbladder (the liver–gallbladder pairing), then pierces the diaphragm, distributes across the hypochondrium (ribs), follows behind the throat up to the nasopharynx, connects with the optic nerve system, emerges at the forehead, and finally meets the Du Mai (Governing Vessel) at the crown of the head. A final branch passes from the liver back through the diaphragm to enter the lung — completing the full circuit and returning qi-blood to the Lung Meridian, where the cycle begins anew.

Classical signs of liver-meridian dysfunction: lower-back pain that prevents bending forward or back, hernia in males, lower-abdominal swelling in females, dry throat, and a dull, "dusty" complexion. The liver's "generated diseases" cover chest fullness, vomiting, diarrhea, nocturnal emission, and urinary retention — virtually all reflections of the liver's two core functions: governing the smooth flow of qi (疏泄) and storing blood (藏血).

### Dao De Jing — Chapter 12

Focus: the cost of over-stimulation and the distinction between nourishing the self and being enslaved by the senses. Five concrete images:

- Five colors blind the eye — not physical blindness, but perceptual saturation: the eye loses its ability to discern what truly matters.
- Five sounds deafen the ear — the hearing drowns in noise, losing the capacity to catch what is worth hearing.
- Five flavors dull the mouth — Wang Bi's note: "爽 means loss; the mouth loses its function." Over-seasoned palates cannot taste the subtle.
- Galloping and hunting drive the heart mad — the mind, scattered by the chase for stimulation, cannot settle.
- Rare goods lead to harmful conduct — the desire for the unattainable warps action.

The chapter's prescription: "The sage attends to the belly, not the eyes" (为腹不为目) — tend to what nourishes the core, let go of what merely stimulates the surface. "Therefore he leaves that and takes this" (去彼取此).

### Kṣitigarbha Sutra — Section 12 (Benefits of Seeing and Hearing)

Focus: the Buddha emits thirty-two types of radiant light from his crown — the most magnificent light-emission in the entire sutra — and announces he will praise Kṣitigarbha's inconceivable benefits. Avalokiteśvara (Guanyin) rises to request the teaching, noting that even the Buddhas of the past, present, and future cannot exhaust the bodhisattva's merits. The Buddha then enumerates specific benefits for different beings:

- Celestials showing the five signs of decay can, by one look or one bow to Kṣitigarbha's image, gain renewed heavenly fortune and never fall into the three evil paths.
- Any being at the moment of death who hears the name "Kṣitigarbha" even once — so that it passes through the ear — will never again experience the suffering of the three evil paths.
- Those who lost parents in childhood may, by painting or enshrining the bodhisattva's image and maintaining devotion for one to seven days, liberate deceased kin from evil destinies; with three seven-day cycles of focused practice, the bodhisattva may appear in dreams to reveal where the loved ones have been reborn.

The section's keyword is "见闻" — seeing and hearing. The bodhisattva's vow-power is so vast that even a single moment of attention — a glance, a heard name, one bow — sets benefit in motion.
